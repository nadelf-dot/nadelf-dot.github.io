Tadabbur Brand Identity

A complete brand identity built from the client's vision — logo system, color palette, and visual language designed to reflect the meaning and purpose behind the Tadabbur name.

The brief

Tadabbur — meaning "reflection" in Arabic — is a sisterhood community built around circles of Islamic insight and learning. The client needed a brand identity that felt warm, intimate, and rooted in meaning: something that would resonate with Muslim women seeking a space for quiet reflection and community. The logo needed to work harmoniously alongside a cloud motif, the brand's original symbol of softness and ease.

My approach

The brief called for something that felt both distinctly Arabic and visually modern. I explored calligraphic treatments of the word "Tadabbur" in Arabic, working toward a form that was smooth, rounded, and fluid — qualities that mirror the brand's spirit of gentleness and reflection. The goal was a mark that could sit comfortably beside or within a cloud shape without competing with it, so I refined the letterforms until the composition felt naturally circular and self-contained.

The outcome

The final logo is a minimalistic Arabic calligraphic rendering of "Tadabbur" — smooth, rounded, and immediately legible. It complements the cloud symbol naturally, giving the brand a cohesive visual language that feels both grounded in tradition and welcoming to a contemporary audience.

MAS Event Campaigns

Print and digital marketing pieces for community events — designed through continuous team feedback to increase visual appeal and grow audience engagement.

The work

Over three years with Muslim American Society, I designed across a wide range of formats and occasions — youth event flyers, camp promotions, lecture announcements, mosque event graphics, merch designs, and monthly calendar overviews. One of the more involved projects was developing a new flyer template for MAS Youth's biweekly program: the brief required integrating updated brand colors while creating something bold and eye-catching enough to hold the attention of a younger audience, all within a consistent and reusable format.

Process & collaboration

Each project involved working closely with the team through rounds of feedback, balancing creative direction with practical needs like deadlines and print specs. Designs evolved through iteration — early concepts were refined based on team input until the final piece felt both on-brand and purpose-built for its audience. Over time, this back-and-forth built a shared visual language that made each new project faster and more cohesive than the last.

Charity Week Global Campaign

Social media design and outreach as US Marketing Lead — collaborating with marketing teams worldwide to create a cohesive campaign with local resonance.

The role

As US Marketing Lead for Charity Week — a global organization dedicated to improving the lives of orphans worldwide — I owned the design and distribution of social media content for the American audience. Charity Week operates across 8 to 10 volunteer countries, with impact reaching communities in regions of urgent need. My role involved coordinating with marketing leads from other countries to align on campaign direction while adapting messaging and visuals to resonate locally within the US.

Design approach

The visual challenge was maintaining a unified campaign identity across a global team while giving the US content its own voice. I focused on creating graphics that were emotionally compelling and shareable — clear hierarchy, strong imagery, and consistent use of the campaign's color system. Collaborating across time zones meant communication had to be intentional, so I made sure every design decision was documented and easy for international teammates to build from.

NASA Visual Identity

Logo design and a complete set of marketing assets for the North African Student Association at the University of Michigan.

Joining NASA

When I joined the North African Student Association at the University of Michigan as Social Media Manager, the organization needed a visual identity that could represent its community with pride and clarity. I was given full creative freedom, which meant the brief was essentially: make something that feels like us. The audience was North African students at a major research university, so the identity needed to feel both culturally grounded and contemporary.

Logo development

I drew on North African visual heritage as my starting point, centering the design on a geometric interpretation of the pyramids — one of the most recognizable symbols of the region. The final mark is clean and minimalistic, embedding the pyramid form within a geometric pattern that gives it structure and visual interest without overcomplicating the shape. The simplicity was intentional: a student organization logo needs to work at small sizes, on social media, and across different backgrounds, so restraint was key.
